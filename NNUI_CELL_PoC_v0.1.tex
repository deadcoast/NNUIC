\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Contemporary UI is authored as a large set of independent boxes, each carrying private
geometry and style. Positions are hand-placed or solved by ad-hoc constraint systems, and
adaptation to new content, new screens, or agent-driven edits requires re-authoring geometry.
We propose \textbf{NNUI\_CELL}, a model that replaces the box abstraction with a single
\emph{continuous medium} whose layout is never authored but always \emph{solved} from local
material state. The medium is governed by two interlocking formal axes. The
\textbf{rheological axis} treats every region as yield-stress fluid governed by a
solidity-modulated Herschel--Bulkley constitutive law with a rate-dependent yield and
thixotropic settling; layout equilibrium (``settled'') is defined as an energy minimum reached
by overdamped gradient flow and terminated by a strain-rate threshold. The \textbf{genetic
axis} treats every region as a cell carrying a \emph{genome} (absorbed data traits) that
deterministically \emph{expresses} a \emph{phenotype} (rendered controls and skin) through an
extensible registry. The two axes share a single interface primitive: a boundary carries an
always-on material-diffusion coefficient and an opt-in, deterministic gene-transfer operation.
We give the constitutive derivations, the discrete real-time solver (XPBD with
viscosity-modulated compliance), a construction for bit-identical determinism, the genetic
operator set (injection, mitosis, symbiotic binding), a compact declarative grammar intended
for LLM/agent emission, and seven executable invariants that pin the design against
reductive drift. We close with four designed proof-of-concept experiments and an honest
account of limitations. This document specifies a system; it does not report a completed
implementation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation: the box model is the wrong primitive}

The dominant UI abstraction is a tree of independent rectangles. Each node owns its box, its
style, and --- through cascade, flexbox, or grid --- a share of the negotiation over space.
Three costs follow directly from the primitive itself, not from any particular framework:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Assets clash.} Two components were never the same substance, so when they meet
  they overlap, collide, or must be manually reconciled. Integration is glue work.
  \item \textbf{Layout precedes design.} The pipeline is scaffold $\rightarrow$ fit $\rightarrow$
  lay out $\rightarrow$ \emph{then} design. Structure and appearance are separate passes because
  boxes hold both independently.
  \item \textbf{Adaptation re-authors geometry.} New content, a resized viewport, or an
  agent edit re-invokes an authoring step. For an autonomous agent this means emitting and
  iterating explicit coordinates --- expensive in tokens and brittle under change.
\end{enumerate}

NNUI\_CELL removes the primitive. There are no independent boxes. There is one continuous
medium, and a ``component'' is a region of that medium distinguished only by its local
parameter field and its skin. Every consequence in this paper is a consequence of that single
move; the rheology and the biology are the two mechanisms that make a continuum tractable and
addressable.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item A \textbf{solidity-modulated Herschel--Bulkley} constitutive law (\S\ref{sec:hb}) that
  folds ``yield stress,'' ``consistency,'' and ``locked'' onto a single continuous field
  $S\in[0,1]$, so that locking is the top of the resistance dial rather than a separate mechanism.
  \item A \textbf{rate-dependent yield} formulation (\S\ref{sec:dualrate}) that resolves an
  apparent contradiction in the design intent --- a material cannot be both shear-thinning and
  shear-thickening --- by placing the rate dependence in the \emph{yield}, not the flow index.
  \item A definition of \textbf{settling} as overdamped gradient flow of an explicit energy
  functional (\S\ref{sec:energy}), terminated by a measurable strain-rate threshold.
  \item \textbf{Viscosity-modulated XPBD compliance} (\S\ref{sec:xpbd}): the bridge that turns
  the continuum law into a stable 60\,fps solver by setting each constraint's compliance from
  the local apparent viscosity. This is the engineering core.
  \item A deterministic \textbf{genotype$\rightarrow$phenotype} expression layer
  (\S\ref{sec:genetic}) with an extensible registry, and a reduction of the biological operations
  (injection, mitosis, binding) to defined, non-stochastic mechanisms.
  \item A \textbf{unified boundary operator} (\S\ref{sec:boundary}) carrying two decoupled
  coefficients, so physical proximity never forces genetic fusion.
  \item Seven \textbf{executable invariants} (\S\ref{sec:invariants}) that convert anti-drift
  intent into automated acceptance tests.
\end{itemize}

\section{Positioning and prior art}

We separate what exists from what we claim is new, to keep the novelty statement honest.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Spring / physics-flavoured animation} (iOS UIKit dynamics, Framer Motion, React
  Spring) animates \emph{between} authored layouts using easing or spring presets. Layout itself
  is still box-authored, and the physics is decorative. NNUI\_CELL has no animation system at all
  (Invariant~6); motion is the solver relaxing, never a tween.
  \item \textbf{Constraint layout} (Cassowary/AutoLayout, CSS flex/grid) solves geometry from
  declared relations. This is the closest neighbour. The difference is the governing law:
  constraint solvers optimize algebraic relations to a unique satisfying assignment; NNUI\_CELL
  evolves a \emph{material} under a rheological law toward an energy minimum, giving it settling
  dynamics, yield behaviour, locking, and content-preserving flow as one continuum rather than
  as separate features.
  \item \textbf{Soft-body / position-based dynamics} (PBD/XPBD in games) simulates deformable
  matter. We reuse the solver but drive its parameters from a rheological constitutive law and
  attach a semantic (genetic) layer, which PBD does not have.
  \item \textbf{Blob / metaball visual effects} are rendering tricks with no layout semantics.
  \item \textbf{Data-driven component selection} (schema-to-widget mappers, BI auto-charting)
  resembles our expression layer but is not embedded in a shared physical medium and has no
  notion of trait inheritance across a shared interface.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Novelty claim (defensible):} \emph{a layout engine whose constitutive law is an
actual rheological equation, in which authors and agents parameterize material properties and
containment rather than coordinates, unified with a deterministic genotype$\rightarrow$phenotype
expression layer at a single interface primitive.} We are unaware of prior art combining these.

\section{The two-axis model}

A region of the medium --- a \emph{cell} --- is fully specified only by two orthogonal axes.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.46\linewidth}p{0.46\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Axis 1 --- Rheological} & \textbf{Axis 2 --- Genetic}\\
\emph{how it flows} & \emph{what it becomes}\\
\midrule
Geometry over time: position, motion, settling, locking, boundaries. &
Identity and manifestation: what data a cell holds and which controls it grows.\\
State: $S,\,n,\,\xi$ (per point); $D,\,\text{bind}$ (per interface). &
State: genome $\Gnome$; phenotype $\Phe=\express(\Gnome)$.\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

the genetic axis alone cannot say where the chart goes or how it settles. The model's expressive

\section{Rheological axis: constitutive model}

\subsection{Solidity-modulated Herschel--Bulkley law}\label{sec:hb}

The classical Herschel--Bulkley (HB) model describes a material that is rigid below a yield
stress $\tau_y$ and flows above it:
\begin{equation}
\tau =
\begin{cases}
\text{rigid}, & \tau \le \tau_y,\\[2pt]
\tau_y + K\,\rate^{\,n}, & \tau > \tau_y,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with consistency $K$ and flow index $n$ ($n<1$ shear-thinning, $n>1$ shear-thickening),
$\rate$ the shear rate.

We introduce a single \emph{solidity} field $S(\mathbf{x})\in[0,1)$ that jointly modulates
yield and consistency, so a single dial spans ``freely flowing'' to ``locked'':
\begin{align}
\tau_y(S) &= \tau_y^{0}\left(\frac{S}{1-S}\right)^{\!\kappa}, \label{eq:tauyS}\\
K(S) &= K^{0}\,(1+\beta S),
\end{align}
with base scale $\tau_y^0$, shaping exponent $\kappa>0$, and consistency gain $\beta\ge0$.
The pole at $S=1$ in \eqref{eq:tauyS} is deliberate: $\tau_y\to\infty$ as $S\to1$, so no finite
input can yield the region --- \textbf{locking is the limit $S\to1$}, not a separate flag.
This collapses the three lock tools into one primitive, \texttt{set\_solidity(mask,$S$)}
(\S\ref{sec:tools}).

For numerical use we adopt the Papanastasiou regularization of the yield term, which replaces
the discontinuity by a steep smooth transition governed by a regularization parameter $m$:
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\text{app}}(\rate,S) \;=\;
\tau_y(S)\,\frac{1-e^{-m\rate}}{\rate}\;+\;K(S)\,\rate^{\,n-1},
\label{eq:etaapp}
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{\text{app}}$ is the apparent viscosity relating stress to rate,
$\tau=\eta_{\text{app}}\rate$. Equation~\eqref{eq:etaapp} is the quantity the solver consumes.

\subsection{The dual-rate response: rate-dependent yield}\label{sec:dualrate}

The design intent contains an apparent contradiction. It asks for (a) rigidity by default;
(b) a fast, accidental impulse to \emph{stiffen} and snap (Oobleck-like, shear-thickening);
and (c) a slow, deliberate press to \emph{soften} and flow (reverse-Oobleck, shear-thinning).
A single flow index $n$ cannot be both $<1$ and $>1$.

The resolution is to separate the two effects onto different terms. Post-yield flow is
shear-thinning ($n<1$: once you have committed, the material gets out of your way). The
impulse-stiffening lives not in $n$ but in a \emph{rate-dependent yield}:
\begin{equation}
\tau_y^{\text{eff}}(S,\xi,\rate)
\;=\;
\underbrace{\tau_y^{0}\!\left(\tfrac{S}{1-S}\right)^{\!\kappa}}_{\text{solidity}}
\cdot
\underbrace{\xi}_{\text{structure}}
\cdot
\underbrace{\left(1+\left(\rate/\rate_c\right)^{q}\right)}_{\text{impact hardening}},
\label{eq:dualrate}
\end{equation}
with critical rate $\rate_c$ and hardening exponent $q>0$; $\xi$ is the thixotropic structure
parameter of \S\ref{sec:thixo}. A slow deliberate input ($\rate\ll\rate_c$) sees
$\tau_y^{\text{eff}}\approx\tau_y(S)\xi$ and, if sustained, exceeds it and flows smoothly (thinning).
A fast flick ($\rate\gg\rate_c$) sees the yield spike by $(\rate/\rate_c)^q$, so the impulse is
rejected and the region reads as solid. This is a rate-dependent yield-stress fluid --- real
rheology --- and it is the honest unification the flat model hid.

\subsection{Thixotropic settling: the ``ooze''}\label{sec:thixo}

Settling requires viscosity to \emph{rebuild} once input stops. We attach a structure
parameter $\xi(\mathbf{x},t)\in[0,1]$ with standard breakdown/rebuild kinetics:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\xi}{dt} \;=\; \frac{1}{T_r}\,(1-\xi)\;-\;a\,\xi\,\rate,
\label{eq:thixo}
\end{equation}
rebuild timescale $T_r$, breakdown coefficient $a$. Under shear the second term dominates and
structure erodes ($\xi\downarrow$, material softens and flows); at rest the first term restores
$\xi\to1$ over $\sim T_r$, raising the effective yield through \eqref{eq:dualrate} until motion
falls below threshold. The visible ``moves around, then settles into the bowl'' is exactly this
relaxation. $T_r$ is the ``ooze'' dial; the design notes' thixotropy dial $\lambda$ is this $\xi$
kinetics.

\subsection{Content as rigid inclusions}\label{sec:inclusions}

Text and images must never shear when their region flows. We model each content payload as a
\emph{rigid inclusion} suspended in the yield-stress matrix: only the matrix deforms; the
inclusion translates and rotates as a rigid body. Formally, inclusion $i$ contributes a
deformation penalty (\S\ref{sec:energy}) enforced as a rigid shape-matching constraint in the
solver (\S\ref{sec:xpbd}), guaranteeing a paragraph moves without smearing.

\subsection{Settling as overdamped gradient flow}\label{sec:energy}

Let $\mathbf{q}$ be the medium's configuration (discretized in \S\ref{sec:xpbd}). The rest state
is the minimizer of a layout energy
\begin{equation}
E(\mathbf{q}) \;=\;
\underbrace{w_f\!\int_{\Omega}\!\big(1-\rho(\mathbf{x})\big)^2 d\mathbf{x}}_{E_{\text{fill}}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{w_c\!\sum_{(i,j)\in\partial}\!\sigma_{ij}\,\ell_{ij}}_{E_{\text{cohesion}}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{w_r\!\sum_i \big\lVert \mathbf{F}_i-\mathbf{R}_i\big\rVert_F^2}_{E_{\text{content}}},
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is occupancy over the container $\Omega$ (so $E_{\text{fill}}$ penalizes voids ---
the slime fills the bowl); $\sigma_{ij}$ is interfacial tension along boundary segment of length
$\ell_{ij}$ (so $E_{\text{cohesion}}$ minimizes interface length, producing coalescence and
menisci); and for each inclusion $\mathbf{F}_i$ is the deformation gradient with $\mathbf{R}_i$
its best-fit rotation (so $E_{\text{content}}$ penalizes any non-rigid deformation of content).
The weights $w_f,w_c,w_r$ are global design parameters.

The medium follows overdamped dynamics --- inertia is dominated by the rheological dissipation,
so we integrate a damped descent
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}\,\dot{\mathbf{q}} \;=\; -\nabla E(\mathbf{q}) \;-\; \mathbf{C}(\eta_{\text{app}})\,\dot{\mathbf{q}},
\end{equation}
with mass matrix $\mathbf{M}$ and viscous damping $\mathbf{C}$ set by the local apparent
viscosity. \textbf{Settled} is a measurable predicate, not a duration:
\begin{equation}
\text{settled} \iff \norm{\rate}_\infty < \varepsilon_{\text{settle}}.
\label{eq:settled}
\end{equation}
Relaxation is asymptotic and never clamped; rendering is cut below a perceptual threshold. The
``alive'' quality is precisely this un-clamped decay --- never a snap.

\subsection{Assimilation as controlled diffusion}\label{sec:assim}

When a new region is injected, its material-parameter field $\phi=(S,n,\xi,\dots)$ blends into
its neighbours by diffusion across the shared boundary:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} \;=\; \nabla\!\cdot\!\big(D(\mathbf{x})\,\nabla \phi\big),
\label{eq:diffuse}
\end{equation}
with assimilation coefficient field $D$. Large $D$: the region melts in within a few frames.
$D=0$: it never blends --- a permanent distinct island (modals, pinned widgets). Note
\eqref{eq:diffuse} diffuses \emph{material parameters}, not content; content is a rigid inclusion
(\S\ref{sec:inclusions}) and does not diffuse. The discrete explicit scheme
$\phi^{t+1}=\phi^t+\dt\,D\,\nabla_h^2\phi^t$ is stable under the diffusion CFL condition
$\dt \le h^2/(2d\,D)$ in $d$ dimensions with grid spacing $h$.

\section{Discretization and the real-time solver}\label{sec:xpbd}

\subsection{Why XPBD}
Full Navier--Stokes or SPH would be accurate and unaffordable, and its results are not needed:
we want stable, controllable, real-time relaxation, not physical fidelity. Extended
Position-Based Dynamics (XPBD) provides unconditional stability, real-time performance on modest
hardware, and --- critically --- a per-constraint \emph{compliance} that we can drive from the
constitutive law. The medium is discretized as clusters of particles connected by constraints
(distance, volume/occupancy, rigid shape-matching for inclusions, interface tension).

\subsection{Viscosity-modulated compliance (the bridge)}\label{sec:compliance}
XPBD solves each constraint $C_j$ with the update
\begin{equation}
\Delta\lambda_j \;=\;
\frac{-\,C_j(\mathbf{x}) - \tilde{\alpha}_j\,\lambda_j}
{\nabla C_j\,\mathbf{M}^{-1}\,\nabla C_j^{\!\top} + \tilde{\alpha}_j},
\qquad
\Delta\mathbf{x} \;=\; \mathbf{M}^{-1}\nabla C_j^{\!\top}\,\Delta\lambda_j,
\label{eq:xpbd}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_j$ is the constraint's Lagrange multiplier and $\tilde{\alpha}_j=\alpha_j/\dt^2$
is its compliance. The central move of this paper is to set compliance from the local apparent
viscosity \eqref{eq:etaapp}:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_j \;=\; \frac{1}{k_0\,g\!\big(\eta_{\text{app}}(S,\xi,\rate)\big)},
\qquad g \text{ monotone increasing}.
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
As $S\to1$, $\eta_{\text{app}}\to\infty$, so $\alpha_j\to0$ and the constraint becomes perfectly
rigid --- the region is \emph{locked}. As $S\to0$ the constraint is highly compliant --- the
region \emph{flows}. Thus one field $S$, through \eqref{eq:etaapp}--\eqref{eq:alpha}, spans the
entire range from liquid to locked, per constraint, per frame. Rigid content inclusions use
$\alpha_j\to0$ shape-matching constraints, realizing $E_{\text{content}}$.

\subsection{The solver loop}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\SetAlgoLined
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{particles $\mathbf{x}$, velocities $\mathbf{v}$, fields $(S,n,\xi,D)$, constraints $\{C_j\}$}
\KwOut{next-frame $\mathbf{x}',\mathbf{v}'$}
$\mathbf{x}^{p} \gets \mathbf{x} + \dt\,\mathbf{v}$ \tcp*{predict (no external gravity; layout forces only)}
estimate local shear rate $\rate$ from the predicted strain\;
update structure $\xi$ by \eqref{eq:thixo}; update $\eta_{\text{app}}$ by \eqref{eq:etaapp}\;
diffuse fields $\phi$ by \eqref{eq:diffuse} (assimilation)\;
\For{$\text{iter} = 1 \dots N_{\text{solve}}$}{
  \ForEach{constraint $C_j$ \textbf{in fixed sorted order}}{
    compute compliance $\tilde{\alpha}_j$ by \eqref{eq:alpha}\;
    apply XPBD update \eqref{eq:xpbd} to $\mathbf{x}^{p}$\;
  }
}
$\mathbf{v}' \gets (\mathbf{x}^{p}-\mathbf{x})/\dt$;\quad $\mathbf{x}' \gets \mathbf{x}^{p}$\;
\If{$\norm{\rate}_\infty < \varepsilon_{\text{settle}}$}{mark region \textbf{settled}; freeze rendering}
\caption{One deterministic solver tick}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Determinism by construction}\label{sec:determinism}
Bit-identical settling (experiment R2) is required for testing, accessibility, and agent trust.
It is achieved, not hoped for: (i) fixed timestep $\dt$; (ii) constraints solved in a fixed order
sorted by stable identifier (Gauss--Seidel, no data races); (iii) all reductions performed in a
fixed, sorted order so floating-point addition is associative-stable; (iv) no wall-clock or
uninitialized state enters the update; (v) where cross-hardware reproducibility is needed,
fixed-point or a fixed float evaluation order is mandated. Under these, identical inputs yield
identical settled states on every run and machine.

\section{Genetic axis: identity and expression}\label{sec:genetic}

\subsection{Genome, germline, sequencing}
A cell carries a \emph{genome} $\Gnome$, a set of typed \emph{traits} (capability tokens such as
\texttt{tabular}, \texttt{temporal}, \texttt{media:video}, \texttt{numeric-range}). Every cell
is born with a \emph{germline} $\Gnome_0$ expressing primitive phenotypes (bare panel, container,
control surface); \textbf{there is no empty cell}, only a cell whose genome is $\Gnome_0$. This
removes any special case for ``empty'' and makes the shipped default UI the same substance as
user-created UI. Absorbing an asset \emph{adds} genes:
\begin{equation}
\seq : \text{Bytes} \to 2^{\text{Trait}}, \qquad
\Gnome \;\gets\; \Gnome \,\cup\, \seq(\text{asset}).
\end{equation}
$\seq$ is a pure function: MIME/structure/column-type detection in, trait-set out. No physics,
no ambiguity, fully testable.

\subsection{Expression as a deterministic registry map}
Phenotype is a deterministic function of genome through an extensible registry
$\R:\text{TraitSignature}\rightharpoonup\text{PhenotypeConstructor}$:
\begin{equation}
\Phe \;=\; \express(\Gnome) \;=\; \compose\big\{\,\R(\sig)\;:\;\sig\in\text{signatures}(\Gnome)\,\big\}.
\end{equation}
A tabular genome expresses a chart phenotype; a video genome expresses a player phenotype.
Extensibility is the whole plug-and-play claim made concrete: a new data type is a new entry in
$\R$, added \emph{without touching the core} (experiment R4). Expression is deterministic ---
there is no stochastic or mutation operator anywhere in the model.

\subsection{Operations reduced to mechanism}
Per Invariant~7, each biological term is admitted only after reduction to a defined mechanism.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\SetAlgoLined\DontPrintSemicolon
\textbf{Injection} $\inject(\text{cell},\text{asset})$:\;
$\Gnome \gets \Gnome \cup \seq(\text{asset})$;\quad $\Phe \gets \express(\Gnome)$\;
set assimilation field $D$ on the new boundary; rheology renders the blend via \eqref{eq:diffuse}\;
\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Mitosis} $\mitose(\text{cell})$:\;
$\Gnome_{\text{child}} \gets \textsc{deep-copy}(\Gnome_{\text{parent}})$ \tcp*{identity copied}
child inherits \textbf{no} position or settled geometry\;
$\Phe_{\text{child}} \gets \express(\Gnome_{\text{child}})$; child re-settles under its own local pressure\;
division visual = one mass pinching in two under interface tension (free from \S\ref{sec:energy})\;
\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Binding} $\bind(A,B)$ \tcp*{deliberate, deterministic}
on the marked shared interface: $\Gnome_A \gets \Gnome_A \cup \tau(\Gnome_B)$, $\tau$ a
deterministic (possibly partial) transfer\;
adjacency alone \textbf{never} triggers this\;
\caption{Genetic operators}
\end{algorithm}

\note{Mitosis is not ``make a copy.'' Taken literally it is genome replication plus independent
re-expression: a duplicated chart dropped into a cramped corner settles compact while its parent
stays wide --- same genotype, different phenotype-in-context. The naive ``duplicate'' verb would
have hidden this correct behaviour.}

\subsection{The unified boundary operator}\label{sec:boundary}
A boundary between two cells carries two \emph{decoupled} coefficients:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Coefficient & Governs & Default \\
\midrule
$D$ (material diffusion) & do the regions blend physical material & always $>0$ (one medium) \\
$\bind$ (gene transfer) & do the regions exchange genome & \textbf{off}; opt-in only \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This preserves the elegance of ``one interface, two jobs'' while ensuring physical proximity
during settling never forces genetic fusion. Symbiotic binding is precisely the deliberate act of
engaging the second coefficient. There is no ambient, proximity-driven, or random gene exchange:
such behaviour would violate determinism (R2) and Invariant~7 and is explicitly excluded.

\section{The agent interface: declarative grammar and token economy}\label{sec:agent}

The end goal is an interface an LLM/agent emits cheaply. An agent declares \emph{material intent
and containment}, not coordinates; the solver produces and maintains geometry. The grammar has
two verb families --- rheological and genetic --- over one region model.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=]
program    := { region | op } ;
region     := "cell" id "{" material content "}" ;
material   := "S:" num "n:" num "xi:" num "D:" num [ "skin:" id ] ;
content    := "hold" asset-ref | "germline" ;
op         := "inject" id asset-ref
            | "solidity" mask num          // t1/t2/t3 all reduce here
            | "mitose" id
            | "bind" id id
            | "contain" id "in" region-ref ;
mask       := "all" | id | "not" id ;      // whole / selection / complement
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent\textbf{Worked example (dashboard).} An agent asks for a wide revenue panel that fills
remaining space with three cohesive KPI cells grouped beside it:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=]
cell main    { S:0.3 n:0.8 xi:1 D:0.6  hold revenue.csv }   // expresses -> chart
cell k1      { S:0.5 n:0.8 xi:1 D:0.4  hold kpi1 }
cell k2      { S:0.5 n:0.8 xi:1 D:0.4  hold kpi2 }
cell k3      { S:0.5 n:0.8 xi:1 D:0.4  hold kpi3 }
bind k1 k2 ; bind k2 k3                                      // cohesive group
contain { main k1 k2 k3 } in viewport
\end{lstlisting}

No coordinates appear. Positions, sizes, the chart type, and the group packing are \emph{solved}.
When \texttt{revenue.csv} changes or the viewport resizes, the medium re-settles with \emph{no
further agent call} --- the zero-cost-adaptation claim. The economic argument is therefore not
merely fewer tokens at generation ($\mathcal{O}(\text{dials}+\text{containment})$ versus
$\mathcal{O}(\text{explicit geometry})$) but the elimination of the re-authoring round-trip on
every change.

\note{Honesty flag. The token advantage above is argued from structure, not yet measured. R3
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}) exists to measure it against equivalent hand-authored markup on three
real layouts; the claim is a hypothesis under test, not a result.}

\section{Invariants as executable specification}\label{sec:invariants}

Anti-drift is enforced by artifacts, not intentions. Each invariant is stated as a predicate that
becomes an automated acceptance test; a build that fails any is, by definition, drift.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em,label=\textbf{I\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{No authored positions.} No code path assigns a coordinate directly; every position
  is a solver output. \emph{Test:} static check + perturbation test (positions change iff material
  state changes).
  \item \textbf{Real constitutive law.} $\eta_{\text{app}}$ is evaluated per frame from
  \eqref{eq:etaapp}; changing $n$ at runtime changes observed behaviour. \emph{Test:} sweep $n$,
  assert response monotonicity; assert no easing/spring preset in the motion path.
  \item \textbf{Locks are solidity.} No boolean lock flag; all locking flows through
  \texttt{set\_solidity}. \emph{Test:} grep/AST for lock special-casing returns empty; $S=1$ region
  is unyielding under bounded input.
  \item \textbf{Threshold-terminated settling.} Settling ends by \eqref{eq:settled}, never by a
  timer. \emph{Test:} varying content mass changes settle time; no duration constant gates it.
  \item \textbf{One medium.} No component owns private layout logic outside the solver. \emph{Test:}
  removing a component type does not remove any layout code path.
  \item \textbf{No animation system.} The renderer is a stateless view of solver state; no tweens,
  timelines, or durations exist. \emph{Test:} freezing the solver freezes all on-screen motion
  exactly.
  \item \textbf{Metaphor reduces to mechanism.} Every biological term in the codebase maps to a
  defined operator (\S\ref{sec:genetic}); expression and all operators are deterministic.
  \emph{Test:} R2 bit-identity holds; no stochastic call in the genetic path.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and threats to validity}

We state these plainly; a PoC that hides them is not professional.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{No implementation yet.} This specifies a system. Every performance and determinism
  claim is a hypothesis with an exit criterion, not a measured result.
  \item \textbf{Token economy is projected.} The agent-cost advantage is argued structurally
  (\S\ref{sec:agent}); R3 must measure it. It may be smaller than argued on layouts where explicit
  control is genuinely needed.
  \item \textbf{Cross-hardware float determinism is hard.} \eqref{eq:xpbd} over IEEE floats can
  diverge across architectures; R2's two-machine clause may force fixed-point, with a performance
  cost not yet quantified.
  \item \textbf{Rheological realism vs.\ performance is untuned.} The regularization $m$, solve
  iterations $N_{\text{solve}}$, and CFL bound trade stability against speed; the operating point is
  unknown until R1.
  \item \textbf{Accessibility is an open design, but a favourable one.} A continuous medium has no
  DOM semantics for assistive tech --- however, the \emph{genome} is a clean semantic layer
  (traits and expressed phenotype), arguably a better accessibility source than inferred box
  structure. This must be designed, not assumed.
  \item \textbf{Continuum cost at scale.} Memory and solve cost for very large interfaces
  ($\gg 50$ regions) is unaddressed and deliberately deferred.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

NNUI\_CELL proposes a single structural break --- \emph{replace the box model with a continuum
field} --- and supplies the two mechanisms that make a continuum both movable and addressable: a
solidity-modulated, rate-dependent-yield Herschel--Bulkley rheology that settles by energy
minimization and locks at the top of one dial, and a deterministic genotype$\rightarrow$phenotype
expression layer that lets a region digest and manifest arbitrary data. The two axes meet at one
interface primitive with decoupled physical and genetic coupling, so proximity never forces
fusion and every biological term reduces to a defined, non-stochastic mechanism. The design is
pinned against reductive drift by seven executable invariants, and its open questions are stated
as four designed experiments with binary exit criteria. What remains is not more theory but the
prototype that will accept or refute it.
